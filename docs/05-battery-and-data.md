# Chapter 5. Battery and Data Tips

*Keep your battery and your data plan alive while using AI. What actually uses your allowance, what doesn't, and the three settings that matter most.*

## The short version

**Typing with AI barely dents your battery or data. Talking sips it. Downloading a local model gulps it once, then costs nothing forever after.** That's the whole chapter in one line — now the details, because "barely" still deserves a few good habits.

## What actually uses data

| Activity | Data used (rough, honest numbers) | Wow-scale |
|---|---|---|
| 20 chat messages (typed) | about 1 MB | a rounding error |
| 10 minutes of voice conversation | roughly 10–25 MB | small |
| One hour of watching video | 500 MB – 3 GB | the real data hog on phones |
| Downloading a local AI model | 1–4 GB **once** | harmless on Wi-Fi, painful on mobile data |

The framing you need: AI chats are tiny compared to the streaming, scrolling, and photo syncing already happening on your phone. If your bill is stressed, your video and photo apps are the suspects — not your AI app.

## What actually uses battery

- **Voice conversation mode** is the heaviest AI habit: it streams constantly, keeps the mic and speaker active. Expect noticeable drain in long sessions — switch back to typing for long Q&A stretches.
- **On-device (local) models** burn processing power to *generate* answers, and older, slower phones feel it. Over an hour of heavy local chat, expect the battery to move faster than it does on cloud AI — but at zero cost outside those moments.
- **Typing in chat** is essentially like any messaging app. Ordinary.

One surprise for many people: it's usually background video, social apps, and location services that eat the battery, not the AI app you just installed. Check **Settings → Battery** (both platforms) to see the real numbers — the list there is ranked and it's often humbling.

## The three settings that matter

### 1. Download local models on Wi-Fi only (if you use them)

Any app that downloads models (offline AI apps, some keyboard features and translate packs) has an option like **"download on Wi-Fi only."** Set it and don't think about it again — a 2 GB model on mobile data is the fastest way to blow a limited plan.

### 2. Background app refresh / background activity

Settings → General → Background App Refresh (iPhone) or Settings → Apps → [app] → Mobile data & Wi-Fi → Background (Android). For apps you only use deliberately — including most AI apps — letting them chat quietly in the background costs battery and data for no benefit. Turn background activity **off** per app; nothing breaks.

### 3. Wi-Fi assist / adaptive data settings

Both platforms have a setting that switches you onto mobile data when Wi-Fi is weak. Useful, but if your plan is tight, leave it: the AI app "barely dents" number above goes up in exactly the scenarios where you're furthest from Wi-Fi (parking garages, break rooms with flaky signal).

## Waiting rooms, parking decks, hospital basements

Places with poor signal are where cloud AI shows a softness: messages fail, or take ages, or voice mode gets choppy. Two tips:

- If you're somewhere signal-poor *often* (a worksite, a facility with thick walls) and you chat with AI a lot there, a local model is worth a second look from Chapter 2.
- Worse than slow is *false quiet* — check the little network icon before blaming the app. Half of "AI isn't working" is just "elevator."

## Sleeping phones and shift work

Nothing AI-related keeps your phone awake at night by default, with one exception: voice conversation mode left open in a tab/app keeps listening and streaming until you turn it off. Close it when you're done. Your battery (and whoever you room with) will thank you.

## A sample day, measured

For one regular person's Tuesday with heavy-ish AI use:

- 15 typed chats on cloud AI through the day: ~1 MB, negligible battery.
- 20 minutes of voice conversation during lunch walk: ~40 MB, a few % battery.
- Camera roll, social feeds, 30 minutes of video: **hundreds of MB**, most of the day's battery use.

The AI usage is a rounding error next to the streaming. That's the takeaway: **use your phone normally, use AI freely, and only spend real attention on the two settings above.**

## Checkpoint

You should now be able to:

- Say which AI habit uses the most data and the most battery (voice conversation mode — for both).
- Find the per-app background-activity setting.
- Explain why a weekend of AI chat didn't move the data bill at all.
