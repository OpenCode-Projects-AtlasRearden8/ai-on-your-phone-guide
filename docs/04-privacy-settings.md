# Chapter 4. Privacy Settings

*Which switches to flip, which to leave alone, and how to keep private things private. No jargon, ten minutes of work, once.*

## The one-sentence philosophy

You don't have to distrust AI to care about privacy — you just have to decide, per conversation, **"would I mail this on a postcard?"** Treat cloud AI prompts like postcards: fine for most things, not for the truly private ones.

## The settings that actually matter

Open your main AI app's settings (gear icon, usually top corner) and look for these. Names vary a little between apps, but these four exist somewhere in every major app:

### 1. "Improve the model for everyone" / training toggle

This is the big one. Most apps let you opt out of your chats being used to train future models. It's usually a switch labelled something like **"Improve the model for everyone"** or **"Use my conversations to train."** Turn it **off** if you want more predictability about where your words go. Note the honest trade-off: with it off, your chats may be kept shorter — apps often auto-delete them after a set number of days.

### 2. History on/off or temporary chats

Most apps have a temporary or no-history mode (a "ghost" or "hourglass" icon near the input box, or a toggle in settings). Chats held this way aren't saved to your account history. Good for the one-off questions you don't want sitting in a list.

### 3. Account and data controls

Look for the section called **Data Controls** or **Privacy Settings**. Useful items inside:

- see and export your chat history,
- delete specific chats (or clear all),
- manage what's connected to the app (email, calendar, contacts) — disconnect anything you didn't deliberately connect.

### 4. App permissions

Your AI app probably doesn't need much. Check **Settings → Apps → [your AI app] → Permissions** and expected minimums:

- **Network**: obviously needed.
- **Microphone**: only if you want voice input (Chapter 3).
- **Notifications**: your choice.
- **Contacts, photos, precise location**: usually *not* needed. If the app asks via a popup, choosing "ask every time" or "deny" is fine and the app keeps working.

Android additional check: in the keyboard settings, mic/voice typing must be on — Gboard's privacy settings are worth a quick look too.

## The details-worth-keeping-private list

As a rule of thumb, keep these out of *cloud* prompts (paste nothing, type nothing, say nothing):

- your full name plus address, birth date, or account numbers together,
- patient, student, or client details — anything about a specific person you serve at work,
- passwords, 2FA backup codes, API keys (if you ever dabble in tech later, chapter 3 of the companion manual covers keys; consumers like you don't need them),
- exact home location ("we're away until the 20th" plus your street name is a postcard you wouldn't mail),
- anything from a document marked confidential, and your employers' internal documents, always — even if the AI *was* allowed, your workplace almost certainly forbids it.

Fine to send freely: general questions, recipes, how-things-work, "reword this email for me" (just delete specific names if you wouldn't post it on a public forum... though honestly, most name-and-email combos are harmless; use your common sense).

## Phone-level privacy (5 minutes, do once)

- **Find My / Device protect**: ensure your phone can be located and wiped if lost. Both iPhone and Android have this under Settings — it's the biggest privacy win of all.
- **App permissions, one pass**: Settings → Privacy (iPhone) or Permissions (Android): a two-minute scroll. Anything surprising (why does *this* app need my microphone?), lock it down to "ask every time" or off.
- **Keyboard**: if you use an AI-powered keyboard, find its "learn from my typing" setting and decide consciously. Offline keyboards (iOS default; most Android defaults) don't upload by design.

## If privacy is travelling with strangers' data anyway...

Just so we're clear where each option sits on the spectrum:

- **Fully local AI** (Chapter 2): nothing leaves the phone. The strongest choice.
- **Built-in assistant on a recent phone**: mostly on-device processing, private cloud for the heavier parts, phone maker promises not to use your content for training. Good middle ground.
- **Typical cloud AI app**: company says what it stores in its privacy policy; your training-off toggle limits reuse. Acceptable with the details list respected.
- **Unknown apps from Chapter 1's copycat list**: treat as the worst option. Delete.

## Deleting your history (2 minutes, do occasionally)

Inside your main AI app: open chats list → long-press or "..." on a chat → **Delete**. For a clean sweep, the "Data Controls"/"Privacy" section has **Delete all chats**. On the account side, if you created an account just to use an app and want out, look for **Delete account** — most apps bury it under Profile → Settings → Account. It's worth the 5 minutes: account deletion removes data the *app* holds, which uninstalling alone does not.

## Checkpoint

You should now be able to:

- Turn off "improve the model" style training toggles.
- Use temporary/no-history chats for sensitive topics.
- Recite the postcard rule and the five things that never go in a cloud prompt.
