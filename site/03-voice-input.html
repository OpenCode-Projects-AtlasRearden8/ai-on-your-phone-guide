Chapter 3. Voice Input

Talking to AI instead of typing — faster, easier on the thumbs, and surprisingly good at catching your thoughts.

Why voice at all

Typing a good question on a phone keyboard is slow and autocorrect keeps "helping." Speaking is roughly three times faster, and it changes how you ask: people say things out loud that they'd never bother to type. Voice input is also a genuine accessibility feature — for anyone with shaky hands, sore wrists, low vision, or one hand occupied with a coffee cup, it's the difference between easy and impossible.

Good news: it's free and built into every phone. No extra apps needed for the basics.

The two kinds of voice input

  1. Your keyboard's mic. On iPhone, tap the microphone key on the keyboard (or use the system dictation setting). On Android, tap the mic on the Gboard keyboard. This turns your speech into plain text in any text field — including the chat box of any AI app.
  2. The AI app's own voice mode. Many AI apps have a dedicated voice button (or full conversation mode) where you speak and it speaks back. This is a conversation, not a dictation line.

Both are reliable and safe to try.

Trying dictation right now (60 seconds)

  1. Open any AI chat app and tap the message box so the keyboard appears.
  2. Tap the microphone key on the keyboard.
  3. Say: "How do you spell restaurant?" — wait to see the words appear.
  4. Tap the keyboard again to stop talking, then send.

That's it. Dictation inserts text anywhere typing does: emails, notes, the chat box. Punctuation: many systems now insert commas and periods automatically as you speak normals sentences; if yours doesn't, just say "comma" or "period" out loud.

Voice mode in AI apps

Look for a button shaped like a waveform, a sound wave, or a headphone with a mic in the app:

  • Tap, speak, release — often a dictation-style toggle.
  • Conversation mode — the app listens continuously, you talk back and forth, and it answers out loud. Excellent for cooking ("what's a substitute for buttermilk?"), driving, or lying flat with an ice pack on your neck.

Conversation mode uses a bit more battery and data than typing — see Chapter 5 for the numbers.

Voice input at work (nurses, teachers, anyone scrubbed in)

This is where voice mode quietly becomes a superpower:

  • Hands busy? Ask out loud between tasks. "Remind me how to word a handoff note" works without touching the screen.
  • Never feed patient information into any consumer AI app. Not a voice prompt, not "just initials, it's fine." Consumer apps are not covered by any medical confidentiality system, and voice input is still input. Use AI for general knowledge only — how things work, how to phrase things — and keep anything about a specific person entirely out of it.
  • After voice input, skim the text before sending. Dictation mishears drug names, street names, and unusual words — a misheard word in a chat prompt is harmless, but the same mistake in an email or note is not.

When voice input trips up

Situation Fix
Noisy room (TV, monitor alarms, kids) Get closer to the mic; hold the phone lower, near your mouth if comfortable
Unusual words or names Spell them out ("A-s-d-a-l-e") the first time, then edit
Accented speech misheard Most systems adapt over weeks of use — keep correcting them; it genuinely helps
Nothing transcribes Check that the app has mic permission: Settings → Apps → [app] → Permissions → Microphone
Sums up fine but inserts wrong words Read before you send — always — and keep errors in the dictation, don't silently accept them

Punctuation and repairs, quickly spoken

  • Say "new line" to start a new line, "period", "comma", "question mark" where your keyboard doesn't do it automatically.
  • If a whole sentence comes out wrong, it's usually faster to clear it and re-say it than to fix word by word.

Talking is also just... good prompting practice

Here's the sneaky benefit: people who use voice input often become better at asking AI for things, because spoken sentences come out as complete, natural requests instead of two-word telegrams. "Hey, can you explain why my phone is asking for a password after the software update, in plain English, like I'm not a tech person?" — try typing that on a bus.

Checkpoint

You should now be able to:

  • Use your keyboard's mic to dictate into any text field.
  • Find the voice-mode button inside your AI app of choice.
  • Name the rule that matters most at work: no patient or client details, ever, by voice or by text.
